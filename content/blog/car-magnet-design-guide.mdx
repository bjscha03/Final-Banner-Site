An effective **car magnet design** answers three questions in one quick glance: **Who are you? What do you do? How can someone respond?** For most service businesses, that means a business name or recognizable logo, a plain-language service description, and one primary contact method.

The magnet is not a miniature brochure. Long service lists, multiple phone numbers, social handles, a street address, a slogan, a QR code, licensing copy, and several photographs compete for the same limited space. Keep only the information a person can understand while the vehicle is safely parked or passing through an ordinary street scene.

Design also begins with the vehicle. Measure and test the exact panel before choosing a magnet size, then build the artwork to that supported proportion. The background must contrast with both the lettering and the vehicle paint, and the full sign must stay clear of door seams, handles, trim, and strong body contours.

## The Best Car Magnet Design Formula

Start with this four-part structure:

1. **Business name or logo** — the main identifier
2. **Service category** — a short explanation of what the business does
3. **One response path** — usually a memorable phone number or short website
4. **Optional visual cue** — one simple, relevant icon or image only when it speeds recognition

For example:

> **NORTHLINE PLUMBING**  
> Residential Repair  
> NorthlinePlumbing.com

Or:

> **SPOKE & WRENCH**  
> Mobile Bike Repair  
> 555-0138

The examples are layout models, not customer stories or endorsements. Their strength comes from hierarchy: one business, one service category, and one response path.

## What to Put on a Business Car Magnet

Every element should earn the space it occupies.

| Design element | Usually include? | Why it helps | When to remove or simplify it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business name | Yes | Establishes identity and supports recognition | Use a shorter approved trading name if the legal name is too long for the panel |
| Logo | Usually | Connects the sign with other brand materials | Simplify an intricate lockup or use a clean wordmark when fine details disappear |
| Plain-language service | Yes | Tells an unfamiliar viewer what the business does | Replace a long service list with one useful category |
| Phone number or website | Choose one primary path | Gives the viewer a next step | Do not shrink both merely to keep both |
| Service area | Sometimes | Can qualify a local business | Remove it if the city list becomes small or crowded |
| License or required disclosure | When genuinely required | Supports applicable compliance | Confirm exact wording and placement with the appropriate authority; do not invent it |
| QR code | Rarely | Can help when the vehicle is parked and safely approached | Remove it when normal viewing involves motion or the code cannot be tested at a useful size |
| Photograph | Sometimes | Can identify a visually distinctive service or product | Remove detailed, generic, or low-resolution photos that fight the message |
| Social handles | Rarely | Useful only when one platform is the intended response path | Remove a row of icons or multiple handles |
| Slogan | Optional | Can reinforce a specific, believable distinction | Remove generic filler before shrinking the service or contact line |

If deleting an element makes the remaining message larger and clearer, the deletion probably improved the magnet.

## Build the Message Hierarchy Before Styling

Write the copy in plain text first. Do not begin by choosing fonts, icons, gradients, or photographs.

### Line 1: the identifier

The business name should normally be the largest or most distinctive element. If the logo already contains the complete name, do not repeat it in a second headline unless the logo is too intricate to function at distance.

Test the name by showing the draft briefly to someone unfamiliar with the company. If they remember the decorative symbol but cannot state the business name, the identity is not doing its job.

### Line 2: the service

Use the words a customer would use to describe the need:

- Mobile bike repair
- Residential cleaning
- Pet grooming
- Lawn and landscape care
- Heating and cooling service
- Event photography

Avoid making viewers decode an internal slogan. “Better Spaces, Better Living” may sound polished, but it does not reveal whether the vehicle belongs to a cleaner, remodeler, organizer, or real-estate company.

### Line 3: the response path

Choose the contact method that the business can remember, monitor, and maintain.

A short website can be easier to read than a long email address. A memorable phone number may be better for a service company that answers calls promptly. If the URL contains several folders, tracking parameters, hyphens, or difficult spelling, use a short owned domain or another response path rather than printing an unworkable string.

Do not add a phone, website, email, and four social accounts merely because they exist. A sign that presents one obvious next step is easier to act on.

## Match the Design to the Actual Vehicle

A car magnet is read as part of the vehicle, not as an isolated rectangle on a white design canvas.

### Test the exact panel first

Confirm that the complete proposed footprint is magnetic-receptive, smooth, clean, and flat enough for full contact. Test the corners, edges, and center; do not assume a whole vehicle model is compatible.

The [vehicle compatibility guide](/blog/will-car-magnets-stick-to-my-car) provides a five-point test for steel, aluminum, plastic, body filler, contours, and repaired panels. Complete that test before investing time in the design.

### Measure the uninterrupted rectangle

Record the clear width and height between:

- door seams and moving panel gaps;
- handles, locks, hinges, and sliding-door tracks;
- molding, badges, and plastic cladding;
- wheel arches and fuel doors;
- strong creases, channels, and compound curves.

Then compare the measured space with the current Banners On The Fly sizes: **18 × 12, 24 × 12, 24 × 18, 42 × 12, and 72 × 24 inches**. The [car magnet size guide](/blog/car-magnet-size-guide) explains the fit and paper-template workflow for each format.

Do not enlarge the design merely because a larger preset is available. A smaller sign with complete surface contact is preferable to a larger one that crosses a seam or lifts over a contour.

### Photograph the door square-on

Take a level photograph of the cleaned vehicle panel in daylight. Place a correctly proportioned design rectangle over the image. This helps reveal whether the magnet conflicts with the handle, looks visually low, blends into the paint, or feels crowded beside trim.

A digital vehicle mockup is a planning tool, not proof of magnetic compatibility or physical fit. Verify the dimensions with a full-size paper template on the actual vehicle.

## Use Contrast That Works on the Vehicle

The design needs two separate contrast relationships:

1. **Lettering against the magnet background**
2. **The magnet as a whole against the vehicle paint**

White type on a dark navy field may read clearly inside the design, but the magnet's silhouette can disappear on a dark blue van. A pale sign can vanish on a white truck even when its internal text is technically dark enough.

Create a one-color or two-color core that stays understandable before adding secondary brand colors. Useful starting relationships include:

- near-black lettering on cream or warm yellow;
- white lettering on charcoal, burgundy, forest green, or deep blue;
- one saturated brand color paired with a neutral field;
- a light border or keyline only when it separates the sign from similar vehicle paint.

Do not treat a thin outline as the only contrast solution. If the overall field and vehicle are too similar, change the field color or add a broader intentional frame.

Review the artwork in grayscale. If the name and background collapse into similar tones, color hue alone is carrying too much of the design. The [CMYK banner color guide](/blog/color-accuracy-cmyk-banners) explains why bright screen colors and printed color should be evaluated separately.

## Choose Typography for a Fast Read

Use type with open shapes, adequate spacing, and enough weight to survive distance and motion. A clean sans serif or sturdy serif is generally easier to scan than a thin script, highly condensed face, distressed novelty font, or several unrelated type families.

No single letter-height rule guarantees readability on every magnet. Vehicle speed, viewer distance, angle, lighting, contrast, name length, font construction, and surrounding traffic all change the result. Instead of relying on a universal formula:

1. build the artwork at the final proportion;
2. print the design at full size when practical, or tile it across ordinary paper;
3. place the proof on the parked vehicle;
4. step back to realistic sidewalk and parking-lot distances;
5. give another person only a brief look;
6. ask what business they saw, what it does, and how to respond.

If the person cannot answer all three, remove content or strengthen hierarchy before making every line slightly larger.

For more distance-testing methods, use the [banner readability guide](/blog/banner-design-readability-tips). The product shape differs, but the principles of message length, letter form, contrast, and viewing time still apply.

## Simplify the Logo for Vehicle Use

An intricate logo can work on a website header or business card and fail on a moving vehicle.

Create an approved simplified version when the primary logo contains:

- a long tagline beneath the name;
- several thin outlines;
- delicate shading or gradients;
- small founding dates or location text;
- a detailed illustration that becomes visual noise;
- a tall symbol paired with a very wide wordmark.

Use original vector artwork when available. A logo copied from a website screenshot may look sharp at two inches and visibly soft at two feet. If the logo is raster-based, calculate its effective resolution at the final printed size using the [banner resolution guide](/blog/banner-resolution-dpi-pixel-size-guide).

Do not save a small JPEG inside a PDF and assume it became vector artwork. File format does not replace source detail.

## Use Photographs and Icons Carefully

One relevant image can speed recognition. Several images usually turn the magnet into a tiny collage.

### When a photo can help

A clean photograph may be useful for a distinctive food product, a recognizable pet-grooming result, or another service where the image communicates immediately. Use an original high-resolution image with one clear subject and enough uncluttered space for type.

### When an icon is better

A simple chainring for bicycle repair, leaf for landscaping, or wrench for mechanical service may identify the category without competing with the business name. Use one professionally drawn symbol, not a row of generic clip-art icons.

### When to use neither

Strong typography may be the most professional choice for a short business name and familiar service. Do not add meaningless geometric graphics merely to fill empty space. Open space helps separate the identifier, service, and response path.

## Decide Whether a QR Code Belongs

A QR code can be tested for a parked vehicle at a trade show, curbside appointment, or jobsite where a pedestrian can approach safely. It is not a responsible primary action for people looking at a moving vehicle.

If you include one:

- keep a plain short URL or phone number as an alternative;
- preserve the code's quiet zone;
- use strong contrast and a flat, undistorted placement;
- test the printed code on the installed magnet, not just on a monitor;
- confirm the destination is mobile-friendly and owned by the business;
- never position the message as an instruction to scan while driving.

The [QR-code banner testing guide](/blog/qr-code-on-banner-guide) explains why scan distance, data density, light, angle, and physical size must be evaluated together.

## Design Matched Driver- and Passenger-Side Magnets

Many businesses order a pair, but the two vehicle sides may not provide identical usable areas. Measure and test each side independently.

The artwork may remain the same orientation on both doors when it contains ordinary horizontal text. Do not mirror the complete file; mirrored business names and phone numbers become unreadable. If a directional icon, portrait gaze, or asymmetrical layout needs to face toward the front of the vehicle on both sides, create two intentionally adjusted files while keeping all text normal.

Name the exports clearly:

- `northline-driver-side-final.pdf`
- `northline-passenger-side-final.pdf`

Proof each file separately. A pair is two placement decisions, not one design duplicated without review.

## Prepare the Final Artwork

Choose the exact supported size before exporting. A 24 × 12-inch magnet has a 2:1 proportion; a 24 × 18-inch magnet has a 4:3 proportion. Switching between them requires a real layout change.

The production file should:

- match the chosen width-to-height ratio;
- use sharp original logos and images;
- keep essential content comfortably away from the outside edge and rounded corners;
- avoid thin borders that exaggerate small alignment differences;
- preserve fonts or convert approved final type appropriately;
- contain no vehicle mockup, door photograph, template labels, guides, color bars, or crop marks;
- use the current accepted file format and size limits;
- show the intended crop and placement in the on-screen preview.

## Car Magnet Design Workflow

Follow this order to avoid designing for the wrong surface:

1. **Choose the exact vehicle and panel.** Record the driver or passenger side.
2. **Test magnetic compatibility.** Check the complete proposed footprint.
3. **Measure the flat rectangle.** Stay inside seams, handles, trim, and contours.
4. **Select one supported size.** Make a full-size paper template.
5. **Write the three-line message.** Name, service, response path.
6. **Photograph the vehicle.** Build a proportionally accurate context mockup.
7. **Choose the field color.** Test it against the vehicle paint and the lettering.
8. **Set the hierarchy.** Make the name and service obvious before decorating.
9. **Add one useful visual cue, if needed.** Remove it if it slows recognition.
10. **Print a physical proof.** View it on the parked vehicle from realistic positions.
11. **Prepare side-specific files.** Never mirror readable text.
12. **Review the production preview.** Check every edge and rounded corner.

## Final Car Magnet Design Checklist

- [ ] The exact panel passed a full-footprint magnet test.
- [ ] The chosen size fits between all seams, handles, trim, and contours.
- [ ] A full-size paper template lies flat on both intended vehicle sides.
- [ ] The business name is immediately recognizable.
- [ ] The service description uses plain customer language.
- [ ] One primary phone number or short website is easy to find.
- [ ] Optional text was removed before essential text was reduced.
- [ ] Lettering contrasts with the sign field.
- [ ] The complete sign contrasts with the vehicle paint.
- [ ] Logos and images come from original, sufficiently detailed files.
- [ ] Important content clears the outside edge and rounded corners.
- [ ] Driver- and passenger-side files were reviewed independently.
- [ ] No text was mirrored.
- [ ] The physical proof passed a brief-read test from realistic distances.
- [ ] The final export contains no mockup vehicle, guides, placeholders, or extra marks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should I put on a business car magnet?

Include the business name or logo, a plain-language service category, and one primary contact method. Add one simple visual cue only if it helps recognition. Remove long service lists, multiple contact paths, and generic slogans before shrinking the essential message.

### Should my phone number or website be larger?

Prioritize the response path customers can read, remember, and use. A short website may be easier than a long email address; a memorable phone number may be better for a call-driven service. Choose one primary path and test it on the parked vehicle.

### What colors work best for a car magnet?

Use strong contrast between text and the magnet background, then make sure the complete magnet separates from the vehicle paint. Dark type on cream or warm yellow and light type on a deep field are useful starting relationships, but test the actual brand colors on the actual vehicle.

### Should a car magnet include a QR code?

Usually not as the primary response method. A QR code can be useful when the vehicle is parked and pedestrians can approach safely. Include a readable alternative, preserve the quiet zone, and test the installed printed code under realistic light and distance.

### Can I use the same car magnet design on both sides?

Often, but measure each panel independently and keep all lettering normal. Never mirror the complete design. Create side-specific versions when an asymmetrical icon, portrait, or visual direction should face toward the vehicle's front.

### How do I know whether my car magnet is readable?

Place a full-size proof on the parked vehicle, step back to realistic sidewalk and parking-lot positions, and give another person a brief look. They should be able to identify the business, service, and response path without studying the design.
