Choosing between **banner grommets, pole pockets, and rope in a welded hem** is really a mounting decision. Start with the structure or hardware that will support the banner, then order the finish designed to connect to it.

Here is the short answer:

- **Choose grommets** when you need flexible attachment points for a wall, fence, railing, frame, tent, or other structure.
- **Choose pole pockets** when a pole or dowel should slide through the banner for a clean, supported top edge, bottom edge, or both.
- **Choose rope in a welded hem** when you want rope permanently contained along an edge so the banner can be tied from its ends.
- **Hemming is not a competing hanging method.** It is the folded, welded edge that strengthens and finishes the banner. Banners On The Fly includes hemming on every banner, including banners ordered with grommets, pole pockets, or rope.

The correct choice is the one that matches the display plan with the fewest improvised connections. A versatile finish is useful, but the most versatile option is not automatically the best-looking or easiest option for every installation.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Grommets** | Metal-reinforced holes placed at selected points along the hemmed edge | Fences, walls, rails, frames, tents, temporary displays | Works with many common fasteners and mounting layouts | Decide which edges can actually be attached and how many points the structure supports |
| **Pole pockets** | Heat-welded sleeves that allow a pole or dowel to pass through the banner | Hanging rods, supported display frames, clean top-and-bottom presentations | Creates a continuous supported edge with less visible attachment hardware | Confirm the pole system, pocket placement, clearance, and artwork safe area |

## First, Understand What Hemming Does

Hemming and hanging hardware solve different problems.

A **hem** is created by folding material toward the back of the banner and heat-welding the layers. This produces a thicker, finished edge. On Banners On The Fly orders, hemming is always included.

A hem can then support one of several finishing methods:

- A grommet can be set through the reinforced edge.
- The fold can be made large enough to form a pole pocket.
- Rope can be enclosed within the welded fold.

That is why “grommets versus hemming” is not an either-or comparison. The more useful question is **which hanging finish should be built into the hem?**

Hemming also affects artwork. The finished edge is a production zone, not the right place for small type, thin borders, faces, QR codes, or essential logo details. Keep important content comfortably inside the banner face and review the live preview before checkout. ## When Grommets Are the Best Choice

Grommets are metal rings set through the banner's reinforced edge. A cord, bungee, zip tie, hook, screw-and-washer assembly, or compatible clip can pass through the opening, depending on the supporting structure and the installation plan.

Their biggest advantage is adaptability. A banner ordered for one event can often move to another location because the attachment points are not tied to one specific pole diameter or display frame.

### Good uses for grommets

Grommets are a practical choice for:

- Chain-link and temporary event fencing
- Storefront walls with suitable anchors
- Railings and rigid frames
- Vendor tents and booth structures
- Indoor walls, approved ceiling systems, and display backdrops
- Banners that will be removed, transported, and rehung
- Installations where several attachment points can share the load

The finish does not select the fastener for you. A zip tie that works on a temporary fence is not automatically appropriate for masonry, drywall, a ceiling grid, or a permanent exterior structure. Match the connector and anchor to the surface, banner size, environment, and property or venue rules.

### Four corners or intermediate grommets?

Four-corner finishing keeps the face visually simple and can suit a smaller banner on a rigid frame with four well-positioned anchors. It is not a universal answer for every large or exposed display.

Intermediate grommets give you more connection points. When the supporting structure allows those points to be used, the banner can be supported more evenly instead of relying entirely on the corners. Ordering closely spaced grommets and then using only two of them defeats that advantage.

Choose the placement from the real mounting surface:

1. Measure the banner area.
2. Identify the usable anchor line on every relevant edge.
3. Confirm that fasteners can be placed without blocking doors, windows, walkways, or equipment.
4. Decide whether the bottom or side edges also need control.
5. Select the grommet pattern that corresponds to those usable connections.

## When Pole Pockets Are the Better Choice

A pole pocket is a sleeve formed by folding banner material and sealing it along the back, leaving a channel for a pole or dowel. That general construction is also described in [Caldera's technical banner-preparation guidance](https://helpdesk.caldera.com/hc/en-us/articles/16602615711121-Preparing-a-banner-technical-and-design-considerations).

Pole pockets are valuable when the display hardware is already part of the plan. Instead of attaching the banner at separate holes, the pole supports an entire edge.

### Good uses for pole pockets

Consider pole pockets for:

- A banner suspended from a top dowel
- A display that uses both a top and bottom rod
- Indoor presentations where visible ties would look distracting
- Photo backdrops and stage graphics supported by a compatible frame
- Retail or event displays that need a straight, composed edge
- Banners that will repeatedly return to the same pole-based hardware

The current Banners On The Fly configurator offers pole pockets on the **top only, bottom only, or top and bottom**. It does not combine pole pockets with grommets in the standard online selection flow.

### Top only or top and bottom?

A top-only pocket supports the banner from above. Gravity lets the face hang below the rod, making it a clean option for suitable indoor or sheltered displays.

Top-and-bottom pockets add a second rod. The bottom rod can add weight and help define the lower edge, or the two rods can become part of a purpose-built frame. The correct setup depends on the hardware; a bottom pocket is not a substitute for securing an outdoor banner against wind.

### Measure the hardware, not just the wall

Pole pockets must accommodate the actual pole and any end fittings that need to pass through the sleeve. Do not order from a visual guess. Confirm the hardware specification and the banner system before choosing this finish.

If an installer, venue, or frame manufacturer provides a required pocket size or construction detail that the standard online options do not address, use the [custom quote page](/custom-quote) before ordering. Do not assume that all rods, frames, and pockets are interchangeable.

### Protect the artwork near the pocket

A pole pocket occupies edge space and creates a fold line on the back of the banner. Keep essential text and logo elements well away from that area. Thin borders are especially risky because even a small difference in visible face area can make them look uneven.

Finalize the finishing plan before exporting artwork. Moving a finished design into a pole-pocket layout at the last minute can crowd the top or bottom of the composition.

## When Rope in a Welded Hem Makes Sense

Rope-in-hem finishing encloses rope inside the folded, heat-welded edge. The usable rope ends extend beyond the banner so the display can be tied to appropriate supports.

This is different from threading a separate loose rope through several grommets. The inserted rope follows one continuous edge and stays with the banner during handling and storage.

### Good uses for an integrated rope

Rope in a welded hem can be useful when:

- The display will tie between two suitable posts or anchor points
- A continuous top or bottom line fits the mounting plan
- The same banner will be installed and removed using the same tie-off method
- Separate clips, hooks, or zip ties would add unnecessary setup pieces
- The banner needs an integrated solution for a temporary event structure

Banners On The Fly currently offers rope placement on the **top only, bottom only, or top and bottom**. The configurator calculates the addition from the selected banner dimensions and placement.

### What rope finishing does not solve

Inserted rope does not determine whether the posts, anchors, knots, or structure are adequate. It also does not eliminate wind load on a solid banner.

Before ordering, identify:

- Where each rope end will tie
- Whether the anchor points align with the selected edge
- How the bottom and sides will behave
- Whether the structure permits tied signage
- Who is responsible for installation and inspection

For an exposed fence or open-air setting, finishing and material should be chosen together. A mesh banner may be more appropriate when airflow is the main concern. Compare the surfaces in our [vinyl-versus-mesh guide](/blog/vinyl-vs-mesh-banners-guide).

## Grommets vs. Pole Pockets vs. Rope by Display Type

| Display plan | Best starting finish | Why | Verify before ordering |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chain-link fence | Grommets | Multiple attachment points can connect directly to the fence | Material choice, fastener spacing, wind exposure, fence rules |
| Flat wall | Grommets | Attachment points can align with suitable anchors | Wall construction, approved anchors, edge support |
| Tent or rigid booth frame | Grommets | Easy to connect with compatible hooks, ties, or bungees | Frame-owner rules and exact attachment locations |
| Top dowel display | Top pole pocket | Creates a continuous suspended top edge | Pole fit, end clearance, artwork safe area |
| Tensioned frame with rods | Top-and-bottom pole pockets | Supports both horizontal edges through the frame hardware | Frame specification and finished visible area |
| Tie-off between two supports | Rope in top welded hem | Integrated rope ends provide a direct tying method | Anchor strength, banner control, permitted knots and hardware |
| Custom proprietary display | None or custom finishing | Standard finish may not match specialized hardware | Manufacturer or installer specification before production |

These are planning examples, not structural ratings. Banner finishing is only one part of an installation that also includes material, size, fasteners, anchors, environment, and the supporting structure.

## Match the Finish to the Banner Material

Solid vinyl and mesh can share some finishing concepts, but they behave differently in use.

Solid vinyl provides a continuous print surface. It is a strong choice when color density, photography, fine detail, and a solid visual field matter. Because the surface catches air, installation becomes increasingly important as size and exposure increase.

Mesh is perforated so air can pass through. It is often the better starting material for open fencing and other wind-exposed placements. The perforations can make small details and color fields appear less dense, so the design should be simplified accordingly.

The finish cannot turn one material into the other. More grommets do not make solid vinyl permeable, and a pole pocket does not make an unsuitable supporting structure safe. Choose material, dimensions, and finishing as one system.

## Common Banner-Finishing Mistakes

### Choosing a finish before identifying the support

“Add grommets” is not a complete installation plan. Know whether the banner will attach to a fence, wall, frame, pole, or tie-off points first.

### Treating hemming as a hanging method

A hem finishes and reinforces the edge. It still needs compatible hardware or an integrated finish to connect the banner to a structure.

### Using only the corner grommets on a large exposed banner

If the order includes intermediate attachment points, the installation should have a reason for not using them. Unsupported spans can leave a few fasteners doing more work than intended.

### Ordering a pole pocket without checking the pole system

Pole diameter, end caps, brackets, and frame clearances affect fit. Verify the actual hardware specification.

### Designing important content into the finish zone

Finishing changes the physical edge. A layout that looks comfortable on a screen can become crowded after a pocket or reinforced fold is added.

### Assuming rope means the whole banner is controlled

A top rope may support the upper edge while the bottom still moves. Decide how every relevant edge should behave in the intended environment.

### Using heavyweight vinyl as a substitute for a mounting plan

Heavier material can feel more substantial, but it does not remove wind or make weak anchors stronger. Material and installation must be evaluated together.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are banner grommets and hemming the same thing?

No. Hemming folds and heat-welds the banner edge. Grommets are metal-reinforced holes added at selected locations through that finished edge. Banners On The Fly includes hemming on every banner; grommet placement is a selectable finishing option.

### Are pole pockets better than grommets?

Pole pockets are better when the display uses a compatible pole or rod and you want a continuous supported edge. Grommets are usually more adaptable across fences, walls, rails, frames, and temporary structures. The hardware determines the answer.

### Can I order grommets and pole pockets on the same banner?

The current Banners On The Fly online configurator allows one finishing method only, so selecting pole pockets clears the grommet selection. If a specialized display requires a nonstandard combination, request a custom quote and provide the hardware specification.

### Which grommet spacing should I choose?

Choose from the usable anchor points, banner dimensions, and environment. Four corners can suit some smaller rigid-frame displays. Closer spacing creates more potential attachment points for larger or more exposed installations, but those points must actually be used with suitable fasteners and supports.

### Do pole pockets reduce the visible design area?

The fold and seam occupy edge space, so essential artwork should not be placed close to the pocket. Keep important content inside a generous safe area and review the final preview.

### What finish should I use on a fence banner?

Grommets are the most practical starting point for many fence installations because they provide multiple direct attachment points. For open or wind-exposed fencing, also consider mesh material and confirm that the fence owner permits the display.

## Choose the Mounting Plan Before the Finish

The best banner finish is easy to choose once the display system is specific. Grommets suit adaptable multi-point attachment. Pole pockets suit rods and purpose-built frames. Rope in a welded hem suits planned tie-offs. Hemming supports all of them by creating a finished edge.

Measure the display area, identify the hardware, protect the artwork near the edges, and then choose the finish. When those decisions happen in that order, the banner arrives ready for the installation you actually intend to use.
