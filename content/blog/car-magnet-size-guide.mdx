The right **car magnet size** is the largest standard size that fits completely on a clean, flat, magnet-compatible vehicle panel without crossing a door seam, body crease, handle, trim piece, or strongly curved area. Measure the usable panel first, then choose the design—not the other way around.

For many car and small-SUV doors, an **18 × 12-inch** or **24 × 12-inch** magnet provides a practical balance of visibility and fit. A **24 × 18-inch** magnet gives a square-ish composition more room for a business name, service, and contact method. Longer sizes such as **42 × 12 inches** and **72 × 24 inches** need a correspondingly large, uninterrupted steel panel and should never be ordered from a vehicle-class assumption alone.

That is the short answer. The better answer comes from matching three things: the vehicle's actual magnetic surface, the distance from which the message must be understood, and the amount of information the sign truly needs.

## Step 1: Make Sure the Panel Is Magnetic

A vehicle may look metallic without offering a magnet-compatible surface. Flexible vehicle magnets need a magnetic-receptive panel—typically suitable steel—with full contact across the back. They will not adhere properly to aluminum, fiberglass, plastic trim, glass, or a nonmagnetic panel.

The simplest pre-order check is a small household magnet:

1. Clean a few possible placement areas.
2. Test the household magnet across the entire intended rectangle, not only one corner.
3. Confirm that it holds consistently at the top, bottom, left, right, and center.
4. Check for body filler, trim, badges, molding, or curvature that interrupts full contact.

The [Banners On The Fly car magnet page](/car-magnets) specifically advises testing the intended vehicle panel before ordering. Magnet manufacturer [Magnum Magnetics also instructs users](https://magnummagnetics.com/clean-and-care/) to apply flexible magnets only to smooth, flat, or gently curved metallic surfaces and to avoid protrusions and concave areas.

Do not assume every door on the same vehicle uses the same material. Test the exact panel where the sign will go.

## Step 2: Measure the Usable Flat Area

Measure the uninterrupted area, not the full door. The usable rectangle must stay inside all of these boundaries:

- Door seams and panel gaps
- Handles and key cylinders
- Decorative molding and plastic cladding
- Badges, decals, or pinstriping
- Deep contours, channels, and sharp body creases
- Rust, dents, peeling paint, or repaired areas

Use a tape measure to record the maximum clear width and height. Then subtract a little breathing room so normal placement does not put the magnet directly against an edge or obstruction.

A low-risk way to check the footprint is to cut kraft paper, cardboard, or painter's paper to the proposed magnet size. Hold the template on the panel and view it from several angles. Confirm that the full rectangle lies flat, looks proportional to the vehicle, and leaves the door handle and seams unobstructed.

### Measure both sides separately

If you want matching driver- and passenger-side magnets, measure both sides. Fuel doors, sliding-door tracks, trim, collision repairs, and vehicle accessories can make one side different from the other.

### Check with the doors open and closed

A template that appears clear on a closed vehicle can overlap a sliding-door path, panel edge, or moving component. Open every affected door and verify that the proposed placement remains safe and unobstructed.

## Step 3: Match the Size to the Message

More square inches do not rescue an overcrowded design. A useful magnetic vehicle sign communicates one idea quickly—usually who the business is, what it does, and how to respond.

For most service businesses, the information hierarchy should be:

1. **Business name or recognizable logo**
2. **Plain-language service category**
3. **One primary response method**, such as a phone number or short website

Optional details should earn their space. A long service list, street address, multiple phone numbers, email address, social handles, slogan, license number, QR code, and five icons can make even a large magnet unreadable.

### When an 18 × 12-inch magnet makes sense

Use the smallest available format when the panel is compact or the message is intentionally minimal. It can work well for a short business name with one service cue, or a recognizable logo with a phone number.

This size is less forgiving of long names and complex logos. If the design needs five lines of copy, the problem is the message—not the font size.

### When a 24 × 12-inch magnet makes sense

This wide horizontal shape suits many landscape logos and short business names. It provides more breathing room than 18 × 12 without adding height that may collide with a door crease.

It is a useful starting point for real estate professionals, cleaners, mobile pet groomers, repair services, delivery businesses, and other operators who need concise identification on a measured door panel.

### When a 24 × 18-inch magnet makes sense

The added height creates a clearer three-level hierarchy: name, service, and contact. It can also accommodate a compact logo mark beside text instead of forcing every element into one row.

Choose it because the panel and design benefit from the space—not because it is automatically “more visible.” A clean 24 × 12 layout can outperform a cluttered 24 × 18 layout.

### When a 42 × 12-inch magnet makes sense

This long format works only when the vehicle provides enough smooth, uninterrupted width. It can suit a wide wordmark or a short service statement on a broad van or truck panel, but it is commonly too long for a passenger-car door.

Build and test a full-size paper template before ordering this format.

### When a 72 × 24-inch magnet makes sense

Treat the largest option as a measured-panel product, not a default fleet sign. It needs a genuinely large magnetic surface with no seams, hardware, ribs, or severe contouring inside the footprint.

If the vehicle cannot provide that surface, reduce the size or consider another signage method. Never bridge multiple doors with one magnet.

## Step 4: Design for Readability in Motion

A vehicle sign is often seen for only a few seconds. The reader may be walking, driving, or viewing the vehicle across a parking lot. Build the layout for quick recognition.

### Use one dominant element

The business name or service category should win the first glance. Avoid giving the logo, phone number, URL, slogan, and service list equal visual weight.

### Use strong contrast

Dark text on a light background or light text on a dark background usually reads more clearly than several similar mid-tones. Test the design at thumbnail size; if the main message disappears, it will struggle at a distance.

### Keep typefaces simple

One strong sans serif or slab serif can carry most vehicle-sign layouts. Reserve decorative type for a short brand word, if used at all. Thin scripts and condensed lettering become fragile when the sign is viewed quickly.

### Shorten the contact information

Use a primary phone number or a short, memorable web address. If a URL contains multiple folders, tracking parameters, or a difficult spelling, it is not a good drive-by message.

### Be careful with QR codes

A QR code may be useful while the vehicle is safely parked and approached on foot. It should not be the only response path, and the design should never encourage people to scan a moving vehicle.

## Step 5: Prepare Artwork at the Correct Proportion

Choose the final magnet size before exporting artwork. An 18 × 12 file and a 24 × 12 file do not share the same proportion, so switching sizes late can crop a logo or create empty space.

Your file should:

- Match the chosen width-to-height ratio
- Use sharp logos and images
- Keep important information comfortably away from the outside edge
- Avoid hairline borders that make small alignment differences obvious
- Preserve adequate contrast between text and background
- Contain no crop marks, template labels, or mockup photography

## Square Corners or Rounded Corners?

The current Banners On The Fly configurator offers no rounding, 1/2-inch rounded corners, and 1-inch rounded corners. The choice changes the silhouette more than the usable message area.

Rounded corners can be a practical vehicle-sign choice because they remove sharp points at the corners. Magnum Magnetics recommends rounded corners for magnetic vehicle signage in its care instructions. Still, corner shape does not compensate for a dirty surface, poor fit, air pockets, or placement across a body contour.

Whichever option you choose, keep essential text and logos away from the corners so the design remains visually balanced.

## Installation and Care Affect Fit, Too

Even a correctly sized magnet needs regular care.

Before installation:

- Wash and completely dry both the vehicle panel and the magnetic side
- Confirm that the paint and finishes are in suitable condition
- Let the magnet reach a practical handling temperature if it has been stored in the cold
- Place it flat rather than dragging it across the paint
- Check that the entire back is in contact with the panel

After installation:

- Remove the magnet before an automatic car wash
- Inspect and clean both surfaces regularly
- Reapply only when the magnet and panel are fully dry
- Remove it after heavy rain, snow, road salt, or visible dirt buildup so trapped debris can be cleaned away
- Store it flat on a clean, level surface when not in use

Magnum Magnetics recommends removing and cleaning vehicle-mounted magnetic signage daily. Follow the care directions supplied with your finished product and consider the vehicle manufacturer's paint-care guidance as well.

## Common Car Magnet Sizing Mistakes

### Ordering from the vehicle model name

“Fits a pickup” or “fits an SUV” is not precise enough. Body materials, trim packages, contours, and repair history vary. Measure and test the individual panel.

### Measuring across a seam

A magnet must not bridge two doors or cross a panel gap. It needs full contact on one compatible surface.

### Filling every inch with information

The goal is not to use all available space; it is to make the important information obvious. White space improves recognition.

### Designing before choosing the size

Changing from 24 × 18 to 24 × 12 changes the proportion from 4:3 to 2:1. That is a layout change, not a simple resize.

### Assuming magnetic means universal

Many visible vehicle components are not magnet-compatible. A household magnet test is faster and more reliable than guessing.

### Leaving the magnet installed without cleaning

Moisture and dirt can accumulate between surfaces. Regular removal and cleaning protect the magnet's contact and help care for the vehicle finish.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the most common car magnet size?

There is no universal best size because vehicle panels vary. For many compact doors, 18 × 12 and 24 × 12 inches are practical starting points, but you should order only after testing and measuring the exact panel.

### Is a 24 × 18-inch car magnet too large?

Not if the vehicle has a smooth, magnet-compatible area larger than 24 × 18 inches with clearance around seams, handles, trim, and body creases. Make a full-size paper template to check the visual and physical fit.

### Will a car magnet stick to an aluminum vehicle door?

No. Flexible vehicle magnets need a magnetic-receptive surface. Test the intended panel with a household magnet; do not infer compatibility from the panel's appearance.

### Should I put the same magnet on both sides of my vehicle?

Matching signs can provide visibility from both directions, but measure each side separately. Fuel doors, sliding mechanisms, trim, and repairs can change the usable area.

### Are rounded corners better for vehicle magnets?

Rounded corners remove sharp points and are recommended by at least one major flexible-magnet manufacturer for vehicle signage. They do not replace proper surface preparation, full contact, correct placement, or routine cleaning.

### Can I leave a magnetic vehicle sign on permanently?

Treat it as removable signage, not a permanent installation. Remove it regularly to clean and dry both surfaces, take it off before automatic car washes, and follow the product and vehicle-care instructions.

## Choose the Vehicle Panel Before the Magnet Size

A good car magnet looks as though it belongs on the vehicle: it fits the panel, sits completely flat, and communicates the business in one glance. Start with a compatibility test and careful measurements, choose the smallest message that does the job, then select the largest supported size that fits without compromise.
