To keep **car magnets from falling off**, apply them only to a clean, dry, smooth, magnet-compatible steel panel where the entire magnetic back can lie flat. Keep every edge away from seams, handles, trim, deep curves, dents, and molding. Remove the sign regularly, clean both the magnet and vehicle, let both surfaces dry completely, and reapply it without trapping air.

Most adhesion problems start with one of five conditions: the panel is not magnetic, dirt or moisture is trapped underneath, part of the magnet crosses a contour, a corner has curled during storage, or an edge was already lifting before the vehicle moved. A flexible vehicle magnet needs full surface contact; it is not held in place by adhesive.

This guide explains how to diagnose a problem, prepare the vehicle, apply the magnet correctly, inspect it, clean it, and store it so the next installation begins flat.

## Why Car Magnets Lift or Fall Off

| Symptom | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Magnet will not hold anywhere on the panel | Aluminum, plastic, fiberglass, nonmagnetic stainless steel, or another incompatible surface | Test a different panel with a household magnet; do not use this location |
| One corner lifts immediately | Curve, crease, molding, dirt, moisture, or curled storage | Remove the entire magnet, clean and dry both surfaces, flatten it, and choose a flatter area |
| Center touches but edges do not | Panel curvature or a magnet curled with the magnetic side outward | Move to a smooth panel and restore flat storage before reuse |
| Magnet slides or shifts | Wet, dirty, waxy, icy, or partially contacted surface | Remove, clean, dry, inspect, and reapply only after the cause is corrected |
| Sign held previously but now lifts | Debris buildup, edge damage, warping, panel change, or improper storage | Stop using it until the magnet and placement pass a complete inspection |
| One vehicle accepts the sign and another does not | Different body materials or panel shapes | Test and measure each exact vehicle panel independently |

Do not respond to edge lift by taping the magnet to the vehicle or bending it around a contour. Remove it and correct the surface, location, or storage problem.

## Step 1: Confirm the Vehicle Panel Is Magnetic

Many modern vehicles contain aluminum, plastic, fiberglass, composite panels, trim, body filler, or nonmagnetic components. A door can look like painted metal and still be unsuitable.

Use a small household magnet to test the **entire proposed rectangle**:

1. Clean the intended area first.
2. Test the top-left, top-right, bottom-left, bottom-right, and center.
3. Move the test magnet across the full area to check for repaired or mixed-material sections.
4. Compare the hold at each point.
5. Repeat on the other side if you are ordering a pair.

A strong hold in one corner does not prove the entire footprint is compatible. The full magnetic sheet must contact a receptive surface.

Magnum Magnetics, a flexible-magnet manufacturer, instructs users to apply magnetic sheet to [smooth, flat, or gently curved metallic surfaces](https://magnummagnetics.com/clean-and-care/) and warns against protrusions, molding, decals, pinstriping, concave areas, aluminum, plastic, and body filler. Follow the finished sign's supplied care information and the vehicle manufacturer's paint-care guidance as well.

### Do not assume vehicle model equals compatibility

Body materials and shapes can differ by year, trim, door, repair history, and optional equipment. A sign that fits one van may not fit another van with the same badge. Test the actual panel—not a vehicle description.

### Be cautious with repainted or repaired areas

Paint, clear coat, wax, and repair materials need appropriate curing and care. The magnet manufacturer publishes approximate curing guidance, but your vehicle, refinishing products, and repair shop instructions take priority. If the finish is new, damaged, peeling, rusted, or uncertain, do not apply a magnet until the appropriate vehicle or paint professional approves it.

## Step 2: Measure a Flat, Uninterrupted Rectangle

The usable space is smaller than the visible door. Exclude:

- Door seams and panel gaps
- Handles, hinges, locks, and sliding-door tracks
- Badges, decals, pinstriping, and protective film edges
- Plastic cladding and decorative molding
- Deep body lines, ridges, channels, and sharp curves
- Dents, rust, chipped paint, and repaired areas
- Any edge where part of the magnet would overhang

Measure the remaining width and height. Then cut a full-size paper or kraft-paper template matching the proposed sign. Hold it against the panel without forcing it to bend. Look across the surface from a low angle; gaps are easier to see when light grazes the edge.

If the template crosses a seam or will not stay flat without pressure, choose a smaller size or a different location. Our [car magnet size guide](/blog/car-magnet-size-guide) compares every size currently offered by Banners On The Fly and explains how to test the footprint before ordering.

## Step 3: Clean and Dry Both Surfaces

Flexible magnets hold best when nothing interrupts contact between the magnetic back and the steel panel. Fine dust, road film, dried salt, pollen, wax residue, grit, and water can create tiny gaps or abrade the finish.

### Clean the vehicle panel

1. Move the vehicle into a safe, well-lit location.
2. Wash the intended panel using products approved for the vehicle finish.
3. Remove grit rather than wiping it across the paint.
4. Rinse away residue.
5. Dry the entire area with a clean, soft cloth.
6. Inspect the panel from several angles for dirt, moisture, damage, or waxy buildup.

### Clean the magnetic back

1. Place the magnet on a clean, flat surface with the printed side protected.
2. Inspect the magnetic side and edges for embedded particles.
3. Wash gently with mild detergent and water unless the finished sign's instructions state otherwise.
4. Use a soft cloth; avoid abrasive pads, harsh solvents, and scraping tools.
5. Let the magnet dry completely before installation.

Magnum Magnetics recommends mild detergent and a soft cloth or air drying. It also recommends removing and cleaning vehicle-mounted magnetic signage daily. Regular cleaning is not merely cosmetic: it allows you to detect lifted edges, trapped moisture, grit, and surface changes before the next drive.

### Dry means completely dry

Do not reinstall over visible water, condensation, ice, or a damp magnetic back. Pay attention to door seams and lower edges where water can continue to run after washing.

## Step 4: Let a Cold or Curled Magnet Relax Flat

A cold flexible magnet can be less cooperative during handling, and poor storage can leave the sheet curled. Do not force a curled sign onto a vehicle and hope the edges settle while driving.

Bring the magnet into a clean, moderate-temperature environment and lay it flat with the printed side facing up. Give it time to relax. If it was stored rolled, the printed side should have faced outward around a broad core so the magnetic side was not stretched into an outward curl.

Do not use heat guns, open flames, clothes irons, or improvised high heat. Excessive heat can damage the printed face, magnetic material, laminate, or vehicle finish. If the sheet will not return to a flat condition or an edge remains deformed, stop using it and ask the sign provider whether replacement is appropriate.

## Step 5: Apply the Magnet Without Trapping Air

Once the panel and magnet are clean, dry, compatible, and flat:

1. Hold the magnet close to the intended location.
2. Align one edge without touching seams, trim, or a body line.
3. Let the rest of the magnet contact the panel progressively.
4. Smooth across the face using light hand pressure.
5. Confirm that the center, sides, and every corner sit flush.
6. Step back and check alignment without dragging the sign across the paint.

If placement is wrong, remove the entire magnet and apply it again. Do not pull one attached edge sideways to reposition the sheet. Magnum Magnetics advises lifting at the sides rather than pulling at corners or dragging the magnet across the surface.

### Inspect from a low angle

Stand near the front or rear of the vehicle and look along the door. A low viewing angle reveals raised corners and air pockets that are difficult to see straight on. Run a clean finger lightly around the perimeter; the transition from magnet to paint should feel consistent.

### Keep edges away from airflow disruptions

A magnet should not touch a door edge, panel gap, handle, molding, ridge, or protruding trim. These features interrupt full contact and can expose an edge. Leave a visible buffer inside the flat panel rather than using every available inch.

## What to Do When a Corner Keeps Lifting

Repeatedly pressing a lifted corner is not a repair. Remove the magnet and diagnose the cause.

### Check the panel shape

Move a straightedge or full-size paper template across the proposed area. A subtle compound curve may prevent the corner from contacting even when the center looks flat.

### Check for contamination

Clean the panel and magnetic back again. Inspect the lifted area for grit, wax, adhesive residue, road film, or moisture.

### Check the magnet's flatness

Lay the sign printed-side up on a level surface. If a corner rises on its own, correct the storage curl before reinstalling. Do not place a heavy object on only one corner; uneven pressure can create a new deformation.

### Check the edge condition

Look for cracking, delamination, stretching, cuts, dents, or a permanently bent corner. Rounded corners can remove a sharp point, but they cannot rescue damaged material or an incompatible panel.

### Choose a smaller sign when needed

If the panel has limited flat space, a smaller magnet that achieves full contact is safer than a larger magnet that bridges a curve. Banners On The Fly offers fixed rectangular sizes and several corner-radius choices on the [car magnets page](/car-magnets).

## Driving, Weather, and Car-Wash Checks

No care guide can guarantee that a magnet will remain attached under every vehicle, speed, weather, road, and installation condition. Inspect the sign before every drive and remove it whenever the placement or material is questionable.

### Before driving

- Confirm every edge is flush
- Verify the sign is on the same tested panel
- Remove any dirt or water at the perimeter
- Check for new dents, paint damage, or body repair
- Remove the magnet if it has curled, cracked, or changed shape

If a corner lifts at any point, stop safely and remove the sign. Do not continue driving to “see whether it holds.”

### After rain, snow, or road salt

Remove the magnet, clean both surfaces, and dry them completely. Moisture and debris can migrate under an edge over time. In freezing conditions, handle flexible material carefully and do not pry a frozen sign from the vehicle.

### Before a car wash

Remove the magnet before automatic, brush, touchless high-pressure, or hand-wash service. Banners On The Fly's current [car magnet buying guide](/car-magnets) instructs customers to remove magnets before an automatic car wash. Store the sign safely rather than placing it loose inside the wash bay or vehicle.

## Build a Daily Car-Magnet Routine

A simple routine is easier to follow than occasional deep cleaning.

### Start of day

1. Check that the correct vehicle panel is clean and dry.
2. Inspect both sides of the magnet and its perimeter.
3. Apply it flat and verify every corner.
4. Take a quick reference photo if multiple drivers share the vehicle.

### End of day

1. Remove the magnet by lifting the sides rather than pulling a corner.
2. Inspect the vehicle finish and magnetic back.
3. Clean and dry both surfaces as needed.
4. Store the sign flat in a clean, dry location.
5. Record damage or panel changes for the next driver.

For fleets, add these checks to the vehicle handoff sheet. Assign responsibility rather than assuming the last person will notice a problem.

## Store Car Magnets Flat and Protected

Storage determines how well the next installation begins.

The simplest approach is to lay the magnet flat, printed side up, on a clean, level surface. Keep it dry, out of direct heat and sunlight, and away from sharp objects. Do not stack cartons or equipment on top of the printed face.

If flat storage is impossible and the product's instructions permit rolling, use a broad core with the **printed side facing outward**. Never fold the magnet, crease it, or roll it tightly with the magnetic side facing outward. Magnum Magnetics specifically advises storing rolled sign material on end with the printed or vinyl side out and avoiding flat spots.

Do not place two magnets with magnetic sides facing each other. Separating them can stretch or damage the sheets. Use clean protective layers and keep each sign identified without attaching labels to the magnetic back.

## Protect the Vehicle Finish

A clean installation routine helps protect both the sign and the vehicle, but every finish has its own history and requirements.

- Do not apply to uncured paint, clear coat, or wax.
- Do not apply over peeling paint, rust, chips, body filler, decals, or repaired areas without appropriate approval.
- Remove and clean regularly so dirt and moisture do not remain trapped.
- Be aware that covered and uncovered paint can weather differently over long periods in sunlight.
- Follow the vehicle manufacturer, refinisher, wax/coating manufacturer, and finished-sign instructions when they differ.

If paint condition or compatibility is uncertain, choose another signage method rather than risking the finish.

## Design Choices That Support Better Installation

Artwork does not create magnetic hold, but the physical design can make inspection and handling easier.

### Choose a size that fits the flat panel

Measure before designing. The message should adapt to the usable rectangle—not force a larger sheet onto a curved door.

### Keep borders away from the edge

Hairline borders make small placement variations obvious and can visually exaggerate a slightly irregular vehicle panel. Leave comfortable internal spacing around the business name, service, and contact method.

### Consider rounded corners

Magnum Magnetics recommends rounded corners for magnetic vehicle signage. Banners On The Fly currently offers square corners, 1/2-inch rounded corners, and 1-inch rounded corners. Corner radius does not replace surface preparation, but it can remove sharp points from the silhouette.

### Keep the message concise

A vehicle sign is often viewed quickly. Use one dominant business name or logo, a plain-language service, and one primary response method. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why does my car magnet keep falling off?

The most common causes are an incompatible panel, dirt or moisture between surfaces, placement over a curve or seam, trapped air, a curled corner, or edge damage. Remove the magnet and check each condition rather than pressing it back down repeatedly.

### Will a car magnet stick to aluminum?

No. Flexible vehicle magnets need a magnetic-receptive surface, typically suitable steel. Many vehicle panels are aluminum, plastic, fiberglass, composite, or repaired with body filler. Test the entire intended rectangle with a household magnet.

### Can a car magnet go over a door seam or body line?

No. Keep the entire magnet on one uninterrupted panel. A seam, molding, sharp body line, handle, or deep curve prevents full contact and can expose an edge.

### How often should I remove and clean a vehicle magnet?

Inspect it before every drive and clean it regularly. Magnum Magnetics recommends removing and cleaning vehicle-mounted magnetic signage daily. Increase attention after rain, snow, road salt, dust, or other dirty conditions, and follow the care instructions supplied with your finished sign.

### Should I remove car magnets before washing the vehicle?

Yes. Remove the magnet before automatic or high-pressure washing. Clean the vehicle panel and magnetic back separately, dry both completely, inspect them, and then reapply the sign.

### Can I fix a curled car magnet with heat?

Do not use a heat gun, flame, clothes iron, or uncontrolled high heat. Let the magnet relax flat in a clean, moderate-temperature environment. If it remains warped or an edge is damaged, stop using it and contact the sign provider for guidance.

## Make Full Contact the Standard

A car magnet stays reliable when the entire system is right: compatible steel, a flat measured panel, clean and dry surfaces, a flat sign, flush edges, regular inspection, and disciplined storage. No single trick replaces those conditions.
