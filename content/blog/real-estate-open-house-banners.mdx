Effective **open house banners and yard signs** work as a sequence. The first sign catches the right driver's attention, the next confirms the turn, and the final sign identifies the property without blocking the walkway, landscaping, or entrance.

Use a large banner when the property has a broad setback, community entrance, fence, balcony, or approved high-visibility surface. Use 24×18-inch yard signs for close-range property identification and directional wayfinding. Keep every message short: brokerage or team name, “Open House,” a simple direction, and the essential day or time when it can be read safely.

The goal is not to put the address, agent biography, phone number, QR code, feature list, and every brand element on every sign. The goal is to create a clear route from awareness to arrival.

## Open House Sign Planning at a Glance

| Location | Best sign job | Useful message | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main approach to the neighborhood | Announce and orient | “OPEN HOUSE” plus one clear arrow | Small phone numbers and multi-line property details |
| Turn or decision point | Confirm the next move | Arrow plus short street or community cue | Ambiguous arrows or signs placed after the turn |
| Property lawn | Identify the listing | Brokerage/team name, “OPEN HOUSE,” day/time | Covering the home, driveway sight line, or landscaping |
| Building or community entrance | Establish visibility from farther away | Large open-house headline and direction | Installing without written permission |
| Front walk or porch area | Confirm arrival | “WELCOME” or “OPEN HOUSE — ENTER HERE” | Blocking an accessible route or creating a trip hazard |
| Interior check-in | Explain the next step | Sign in, shoe-cover request, or tour direction | Repeating roadside marketing copy |

Every placement must be legal and authorized. Sign ordinances, association rules, brokerage standards, event timing limits, public right-of-way restrictions, and landlord or community policies vary. Confirm the applicable requirements for each listing instead of copying the previous property's placement plan.

## Build the Route Before You Design the Signs

Start with a map and drive the route a visitor will actually use.

Mark:

- The primary approach from the busiest relevant road
- Every turn where a visitor could become uncertain
- Medians, divided roads, one-way streets, gates, and restricted entrances
- Safe sign locations on property you are authorized to use
- Existing visual clutter that may hide a sign
- Sidewalks, curb ramps, driveways, utilities, and landscaping
- The final arrival point and preferred entrance

Then assign one job to each sign. If two signs at the same location say different things, a moving driver has to choose which one to process. Simplify the system until each decision point has one obvious instruction.

### Place the cue before the decision

A direction sign that becomes readable only after the turn is not useful. Stand where the visitor will approach and check whether the arrow is visible early enough to understand. Account for parked vehicles, seasonal foliage, utility poles, and other signs.

### Use confirmation signs

After a major turn or gate, a small confirmation sign reassures visitors that they are still on the correct route. This is especially useful in large subdivisions, apartment communities, rural drives, and developments with several similar streets.

### Keep the property sign distinct

The final sign should look related to the route signs but clearly signal arrival. A large “OPEN HOUSE” line, brokerage identity, and restrained welcome message are usually more useful than another arrow.

## Choose Between a Banner and Yard Signs

### Yard signs are best for repeated wayfinding

Banners On The Fly's online yard-sign product is a fixed 24×18-inch corrugated-plastic rectangle, available in quantities of 10 with optional step stakes. That format is suited to a coordinated set of property and directional signs when the message can be designed to work within the fixed space.

Use yard signs for:

- Turn-by-turn arrows
- Neighborhood entrance confirmation
- Property identification at lawn level
- “Open today” or “enter here” messages
- Reusable brokerage-branded direction sets

Review the current format and options on the [yard sign product page](/yard-signs) before designing. Do not plan custom dimensions that the online configurator does not offer.

### Banners are best for larger viewing areas

A custom vinyl banner can make sense when:

- The home or venue sits far back from the road
- The listing is inside a large approved community entrance
- A fence or building surface offers a safe, authorized mounting area
- The event includes several units, a development launch, or a model-home center
- The message must be understood across a parking area

Large banners require a complete installation plan. Confirm the surface, attachment method, visibility, wind exposure, and property permission. ## Write Messages for Moving and Stationary Viewers

### Roadside message

The driver-facing message should be extremely short:

> **OPEN HOUSE**<br />
> →

or

> **OAK & MAIN REALTY**<br />
> OPEN HOUSE →

Do not ask a moving driver to read a paragraph, copy a phone number, or scan a QR code. Contact details can appear on the property sign where visitors can stop safely.

### Property message

At the lawn, visitors have more time:

> **OAK & MAIN REALTY**<br />
> OPEN HOUSE<br />
> Sunday 1–3

This version identifies the brokerage, confirms the event, and provides the current window without filling the sign with listing details.

### Entrance message

Near the building:

> **WELCOME**<br />
> Please enter through the front door

Use interior or entrance signs for instructions that matter only after arrival.

## Make the Arrow Impossible to Misread

Directional arrows fail when they are too small, stylized, or surrounded by equally strong graphics.

Use:

- A simple, bold arrow shape
- Strong contrast against the background
- Generous clear space around the arrow
- The same arrow style throughout the route
- Separate left- and right-arrow files rather than rotating the physical sign unpredictably

Print a route map showing which arrow belongs at each location. Label the back of every sign so setup does not depend on memory.

Avoid diagonal arrows unless the decision truly requires one. At many intersections, a clean left or right arrow is faster to understand.

## Design a Reusable Sign System

Reusable signs save setup time only when variable information has been planned carefully.

### Keep the permanent layer broad

The reusable design can include:

- Brokerage or team name
- Brand colors
- Website or office contact, when appropriate
- “Open House”
- Directional arrow

### Keep changing details off route signs

Dates, property addresses, agent names, and time windows change. If each directional sign includes listing-specific information, the entire set becomes single-use.

A more durable system uses generic direction signs along the route and one property-specific sign at the destination. If a temporary date panel or insert is used, make sure it is secure, weather-appropriate, and readable.

### Create a setup inventory

Maintain a simple kit:

- Left arrows
- Right arrows
- Straight-ahead confirmations
- Property identification signs
- Entrance/welcome signs
- Correct number of compatible stakes
- Location map and pickup checklist

Inspect every sign and stake after use. A bent or loose support should not be returned to the kit as if it is ready.

## Protect Readability at Real-World Distances

A 24×18-inch yard sign has limited space. Treat it like a roadside cue, not a miniature listing flyer.

### Use a decisive hierarchy

1. **Event:** OPEN HOUSE
2. **Direction or identity:** arrow or brokerage name
3. **Optional detail:** day and short time window

### Keep type simple

Use a bold typeface with open letterforms. Avoid thin scripts for the main event message, tightly condensed fonts, or all-caps paragraphs.

### Test at distance

Print the critical words at actual size, place them at the intended distance, and look for several seconds—not several minutes. Test from the angle of approach and inside a parked vehicle when safe.

### Separate the sign from its environment

Green text on a green lawn, beige on winter grass, or low-contrast gray near a concrete driveway can disappear. Use a clear border or strong background block when the surrounding scene is visually busy.

## Place Yard Signs in Suitable Ground

A step stake belongs in suitable soil—not concrete, asphalt, a sidewalk, a curb joint, artificial turf, a planter box, or a location where the installer has not checked for restrictions and underground hazards.

Before insertion:

1. Confirm you have permission to use the location.
2. Check local rules and any utility or irrigation concerns.
3. Choose stable, appropriate ground away from walkways and vehicle paths.
4. Insert the stake evenly; do not force it into an unknown obstruction.
5. Mount the sign level and verify it does not spin or lean.
6. View it from the intended approach.

Do not place signs where they reduce driveway sight distance or make pedestrians move around them.

The [ADA Standards](https://www.ada.gov/law-and-regs/design-standards/2010-stds/) require accessible routes in covered facilities and sites. An open-house sign plan should preserve clear paths rather than treating sidewalks, curb ramps, accessible parking access aisles, or entrances as spare display space.

## Plan for Agent and Visitor Safety

Signage is a marketing tool, not a complete safety plan. A clear route may bring more visitors, including people the agent has never met.

The National Association of REALTORS® recommends open-house safety practices such as working with a buddy, checking in with the office, requesting identification, limiting the number of people inside, and protecting access codes. Review the current [NAR open-house resources](https://www.nar.realtor/open-houses) and your brokerage procedures before the event.

Practical sign-related safety steps include:

- Have two people place or retrieve signs when traffic or unfamiliar locations create risk
- Do not stop a vehicle in a travel lane to install a sign
- Wear visible clothing and remain aware of traffic
- Keep the route sheet and open-house address with the team
- Collect signs before dark when practical
- Do not publish private lockbox, access, or security information on public signage
- Inventory every sign after the event so nothing remains on unauthorized property

## Coordinate the Banner With Digital Marketing

The physical sign and online listing should use the same recognizable identity, event time, and property naming. A visitor who saw a social post should recognize the lawn sign immediately.

QR codes can be useful on the destination sign when a stationary visitor can scan safely. Link to a mobile-friendly property page, not a generic homepage. Test the exact printed code at the final size before deployment and keep a plain-language URL or agent contact available as a fallback.

Do not place QR codes on high-speed roadside signs or make them the only way to understand the event.

## Open House Sign Timeline

### One to two weeks before

- Confirm local, HOA, community, and brokerage rules
- Drive and map the visitor route
- Count decision points and approved locations
- Choose banner and yard-sign roles
- Finalize the reusable and listing-specific copy
- Verify event date and time

### Before ordering

- Confirm the 24×18 yard-sign format and quantity requirements
- Confirm optional stake quantity
- Check the banner's finished dimensions and mounting plan
- Proof left, right, and straight arrow files independently
- Review names, phone numbers, URL, date, and time
- Inspect uploaded previews at every edge

### Event day

- Follow the route map in order
- Photograph each placement for pickup
- Test visibility from the actual approach
- Keep sidewalks, curb ramps, driveways, and entrances clear
- Reposition only where rules and permission allow

### After the event

- Remove every temporary sign promptly
- Clean and dry signs before storage
- Inspect stakes and printed surfaces
- Record which placements worked
- Correct the route map for the next event

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should an open house yard sign say?

Lead with “Open House,” then add one clear arrow or the brokerage identity. At the property, include the short day and time window if it remains easy to read. Avoid turning directional signs into listing flyers.

### How many open house signs do I need?

Count the approved decision points on the primary route, then add the property and entrance signs. The number depends on road layout, community rules, distance, and how easily a first-time visitor can find the home.

### What size yard signs does Banners On The Fly sell online?

The supported online yard-sign format is 24×18 inches in corrugated plastic, with single- or double-sided printing and optional step stakes. Quantities and current options are shown in the configurator.

### Where should I place open house signs?

Use authorized locations before turns, at confusing decision points, and at the property. Keep signs off public or private property where permission is absent and away from traffic sight lines, sidewalks, accessible routes, and underground hazards.

### Should I put a QR code on an open house sign?

Use one only where a visitor can stop safely, such as the property lawn or check-in area. Test it at final print size and provide a readable fallback. Do not expect moving drivers to scan it.

### Can open house signs be reused?

Yes. Keep route signs generic, use consistent arrows and branding, and reserve listing-specific details for the destination sign or a secure changeable element. Clean, inspect, inventory, and store the set after every event.

## Create a Route People Can Follow

A successful open-house sign system reduces uncertainty one decision at a time. Use large, short messages for moving viewers, preserve detailed information for the destination, and place every sign with permission, visibility, access, and retrieval in mind.
